## 1. How to use this guide

"Automation" simply means your Claude does the repetitive work for you, and you only step in to approve things or handle the exceptions it cannot deal with. Think of it like a very capable first officer. It flies the routine legs, keeps you informed, and hands control back to you the moment something is out of the ordinary.

Here is the important part: you do not set all of this up at once. You turn on one automation at a time. Your Claude walks you through each one, gets it working, and only then do you move to the next. If any automation ever feels like too much, you can pause it or switch it back to "ask me first" mode.

How to work through this guide:

- Read it once, top to bottom, so you know what is possible. Do not worry about remembering the details.
- When you are ready to start, open Claude and say what you want in your own words. There are ready-made phrases in this guide (shown in grey boxes) that you can copy word for word.
- Let Claude do the setup. It will ask you questions when it needs a decision from you.
- Start small. Get one thing working and trusted before adding the next.

A note for Ray's Claude: treat this document as the brief. Set up automations gradually, one at a time, confirming each works before moving on. Always keep a human (Ray) in the loop for anything sensitive, and follow the "never answer on its own" list in the email section. Australian spelling, AUD, and no em-dashes or en-dashes in anything you produce for Ray.

## 2. What "running 24/7" really needs (the honest version)

A lot of automation is about things happening on a schedule, including while you are asleep or flying. To be straight with you: a program can only run when a computer is awake to run it. This matters, so here is the plain truth.

Your laptop is not enough on its own. When your laptop goes to sleep, closes, or runs out of battery, everything pauses. So a task set for 6am will not run if the lid is shut. That is not a fault, it is just how computers work.

To have things happen reliably around the clock, you need one of these two options:

- Option A: an always-on computer. A small, cheap computer that stays switched on and awake all the time, sitting quietly in a corner of your home or office. A Mac Mini is the popular choice because it is small, quiet, cheap to run, and very reliable. This is the option most serious small businesses use, because it just works. You leave it on, and your scheduled tasks run on time, every time.
- Option B: cloud-based scheduling. Instead of your own computer, the task runs on a computer somewhere on the internet ("the cloud") that is always on. Some tools offer this. It can be simpler because there is no hardware to look after, though it can have monthly costs and a bit more setup.

For most of what you want (a social scheduler posting for you, an email agent checking your inbox, weekly reports), here is the good news: the social scheduler and many email tools already live in the cloud and run on their own. So you may not need an always-on machine straight away. You would mainly want one once you have several custom scheduled tasks that must run at exact times without you around.

What your Claude can do: it can set up scheduled tasks that run automatically at the times you choose. Just know that those scheduled tasks need a computer that is awake to run them, which is why the always-on machine or the cloud matters for true 24/7.

> Claude, explain in simple terms whether my current setup can run tasks while my laptop is asleep, and what my options are for an always-on machine or cloud scheduling.

## 3. Social media autopilot (your advertising engine)

This is the big one you asked for: advertising posts going out automatically across all your social media. Here is the reliable, safe, ban-proof way real businesses do it.

### The idea in one sentence

Claude writes your posts, you approve them, and an official scheduling tool publishes them automatically to every platform at the best times.

### The three parts

1. Claude creates the posts. Claude writes on-brand posts for you: the caption, the hook (the first line that grabs attention), hashtags, and ideas for the image or video. It tailors them to your audiences, especially pilots, but also the other high-stakes professionals you train. It can produce posts for all the main platforms:
   - Facebook
   - Instagram
   - TikTok
   - LinkedIn (strong for reaching pilots, airlines, and corporate training buyers)
   - YouTube (Shorts)
   - Pinterest
   - X (formerly Twitter)

2. An official scheduler publishes them. This is the safe part. Rather than a bot logging into your accounts and pretending to be you (which can get your accounts suspended or banned), the posts go out through an official, approved scheduling tool that the platforms themselves support. Your accounts stay safe.
   - Recommended: Metricool. It has a free plan, it is official and approved by the platforms, and it connects to all the networks above. This is the ban-proof option.
   - Alternatives: Buffer and Later. Both are also official and well known. Any of the three is fine. Metricool is suggested first because of its free plan and wide platform support.

3. A human approves before anything goes out. Claude never logs into or operates your social accounts directly. It prepares a batch of posts, you (or Gregory) look them over, and only the approved ones get scheduled. This keeps you in control and keeps you safe.

### The workflow, start to finish

- On a schedule (say once a week), Claude writes a batch of posts for the week ahead.
- You review the batch. Edit anything, remove anything, approve the rest. This takes a few minutes.
- The approved posts are loaded into the scheduler (Metricool).
- The scheduler posts them automatically to every platform at the best times, even while you are asleep or flying.

Because the scheduler is doing the actual posting, this genuinely runs without you once it is set up. Your only ongoing job is the quick weekly approval.

### Staying on the right side of advertising rules (high level only)

You are advertising a real business, so a few honest-advertising basics apply under Australian Consumer Law. At a high level:

- Do not make misleading or exaggerated claims (for example about results, cures, or guarantees).
- Any testimonials or reviews you use must be genuine and from real people.
- Be clear that you are the business doing the advertising (do not pretend a promotional post is independent or unpaid when it is yours).

This is general information, not legal advice. For anything specific, especially claims about psychological or performance outcomes, confirm with a qualified professional before you publish.

### A simple starting cadence

Do not try to be everywhere at full volume on day one. A comfortable start:

- Two or three posts per platform per week.
- Begin with just the one or two platforms where your audience actually is (LinkedIn and Facebook are a sensible first pair for pilots and corporate training).
- Once it is running smoothly and you trust the quality, your Claude can scale it up: more platforms, more posts, more variety.

Phrases you can say to your Claude:

> Help me set up the social media autopilot, starting with LinkedIn and Facebook.

> Claude, write me a week of posts for Human Ballistics aimed at pilots, and show me the batch to approve before anything is scheduled.

> Claude, walk me through connecting Metricool so approved posts publish automatically.

## 4. Email agent (Claude answers routine email, escalates the hard ones)

This lets Claude handle your everyday email so your inbox stops eating your time, while making sure anything important still comes to you.

### How it works

- Set up a business support inbox, something like hello@humanballistics.com or info@humanballistics.com. This is the address customers use for questions, and the one Claude watches. Keep it separate from your personal email.
- Claude reads incoming emails. For routine, everyday questions (course dates, what is included, how to book, how the membership works, where to find a recorded lesson), Claude writes a friendly, on-brand reply using your FAQ and knowledge base as its source of truth (see section 5).
- For anything it is not sure about, Claude does not answer. Instead it flags the email and notifies you so you can reply yourself. This human-in-the-loop step is the safety net. Claude only handles what it can clearly and correctly handle.

### Start in "draft for review" mode

When you first switch this on, run it in draft mode:

- Claude reads the email and writes a suggested reply.
- You read the draft, tweak it if you want, and send it (or approve it to be sent).

This lets you see the quality and build trust. Once you are consistently happy with the routine drafts, you can let Claude auto-send those routine replies on its own, while still flagging everything unusual to you. Move to auto-send gradually, and only for the simple, repetitive questions.

### Things Claude must NEVER answer on its own (always escalate to Ray)

This is a firm list. For any of these, Claude flags the email and hands it to you, no matter how confident it might seem:

- Pricing disputes or complaints about money.
- Refunds, cancellations, or chargebacks.
- Medical, psychological, or mental-health advice (including anything about fear, anxiety, trauma, or a person's wellbeing). This is central to your business and must always be handled by you.
- Legal questions, threats, or anything involving liability.
- Anything about a specific student's or client's personal situation or progress.
- Founder or personal questions (anything that really needs Ray's own voice or judgement).
- Anything Claude is simply unsure about.

When in doubt, Claude escalates. It is always better to pass an email to you than to guess.

Phrases you can say to your Claude:

> Help me set up the email agent in draft mode, using my FAQ as the source of truth.

> Claude, from now on draft replies to routine emails and flag anything about refunds, medical or psychological topics, legal issues, or a specific client, so I can handle those myself.

> Claude, I have watched your drafts for a while and I trust them. Let's move the simplest, most routine replies to auto-send, but keep flagging anything unusual.

## 5. FAQ and knowledge base (the brain behind the email agent)

Your website now has an FAQ page. That FAQ does double duty: it is also the knowledge base that your email agent answers from. In other words, the answers customers can read on your site are the exact same answers Claude uses when replying to emails. One source of truth, kept in one place.

Why this matters:

- It keeps your email replies accurate and consistent with your website.
- It gets smarter over time. Whenever a customer asks something new and you answer it, Claude can add that question and answer to the FAQ and knowledge base. The next time anyone asks, Claude already knows.

Keeping the knowledge base current is itself a small automation. Your Claude can watch for new questions coming through email, note the ones you had to answer yourself, and suggest adding them to the FAQ so the agent keeps improving.

Phrases you can say to your Claude:

> Claude, add this question and the answer I just gave to the FAQ and knowledge base.

> Claude, review the emails I had to answer myself this month and suggest new FAQ entries so you can handle them next time.

## 6. Automate everything else

Once the email agent and social autopilot are running, here is a menu of other useful automations your Claude can set up. You do not need all of these. Pick the ones that would actually save you time or make you money.

- Booking reminders: automatic reminder emails before a course or coaching session so people show up.
- Post-course follow-ups: a friendly thank-you and next-steps message after someone finishes the 2-day Performance Course or a Fly Calm program.
- Monthly newsletter: Claude drafts a monthly email to your list, you approve, it sends.
- Membership welcome sequence: a series of welcome emails that guide new members through the Zoom sessions and recorded lessons in their first weeks.
- New lesson notifications: when you add a new recorded lesson to the members area, members get told automatically.
- Weekly performance report: a short weekly summary of your numbers (bookings, new members, website traffic) so you always know how the business is tracking.
- Review requests: automatic, gentle requests for a review or testimonial from past course participants (remember: testimonials must be genuine).
- Keeping the website fresh: Claude can help keep pages, dates, and content up to date so nothing goes stale.

Phrases you can say to your Claude:

> Claude, which of these extra automations would save me the most time given how Human Ballistics runs? Suggest three to start with.

> Claude, set up a post-course follow-up email for people who finish the 2-day Performance Course.

## 7. A simple rollout order (so you are not overwhelmed)

Do these in order. Get each one comfortable before moving on.

1. First: the email agent in draft mode. It saves you time immediately, and draft mode means zero risk. You approve every reply while you build trust.
2. Second: the social media autopilot. Start with one or two platforms (LinkedIn and Facebook), a few posts a week, with you approving each batch.
3. Third: the knowledge base habit. As real questions come in, keep feeding new answers into the FAQ so the email agent gets smarter.
4. After that, pick and choose: add booking reminders, the monthly newsletter, the membership welcome sequence, or weekly reports, one at a time, whenever you are ready.

You are never locked in. Any automation can be paused, slowed down, or switched back to "ask me first" whenever you want.

> Claude, let's start the rollout. Set up the email agent in draft mode first, and check with me before we move to the social autopilot.
